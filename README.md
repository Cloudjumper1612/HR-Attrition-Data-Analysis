HR Employee Attrition & Performance Analysis

Business Problem
Employee turnover (attrition) is a major cost for businesses. This project analyzes a dataset of employee demographics, compensation, and job roles to identify the root causes of why employees leave and to build a profile of "high flight-risk" individuals.

Tools & Stack Used:
Data Cleaning & Prototyping:  Microsoft Excel 
Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA): Pivot Tables & Aggregations
Querying & Logic: SQL (Script included in repo)

Key Business Insights
During the exploratory data analysis phase, several critical trends were uncovered:
The Compensation Gap: Employees who left the company earned roughly 30% less on average than those who stayed (R4,787 vs R6,832).
The Overtime Burnout: Employees forced to work overtime have an attrition rate of 30.5%, compared to just 10.4% for those who do not.
The High-Risk Role: The Sales Representative role experiences a massive **39.7%** turnover rate, the highest in the company.

The "Perfect Storm" (Flight-Risk Persona)
By combining multiple variables, I identified a specific failure point in retention. If an employee fits the following criteria:
1. Works as a Sales Representative
2. Works Overtime
3. Has a commute longer than 10 miles
Their attrition rate skyrockets to 83.3%.

Strategic Recommendations
Targeted Salary Reviews: HR should conduct immediate compensation reviews for Sales Representatives.
Commute & Remote Work: Offer flexible or hybrid remote days for employees with commutes longer than 10 miles who are required to work overtime.
